Kenya Didn't Beat Nigeria. The Naira Did.

Kenya topped African tech funding in 2025, but almost three-quarters of its lead over Nigeria was debt, and debt follows currency. The CBN sets rates again this week. The gap that investors read as a verdict on the market may just be FX data arriving late.

Partech's 2025 report ranked Kenya first in African tech funding with US$1.04 billion, up 72%. Nigeria came fourth with US$572 million, down 3%. The headline wrote itself: capital has moved east.

It hasn't, or not in the way the ranking implies. Split the numbers and Kenya's lead turns out to be a statement about the shilling more than about Nairobi.

Split the Stack

Debt made up 48% of the capital deployed in Kenya in 2025, about US$498 million. Nigeria raised roughly US$160 million in debt. Subtract debt from each total and equity comes out at about US$540 million for Kenya against about US$412 million for Nigeria. Nigeria also closed more deals than any other market: 102, against Kenya's 91.

That puts the total gap at about US$468 million, and about US$338 million of it is debt. Roughly 72% of Kenya's lead is credit, not conviction. And the credit is concentrated: four transactions, about US$610 million, made up 60% of everything Kenya raised.

One year earlier the ranking ran the other way. In its 2024 report Partech had Nigeria back on top, with US$520 million in equity (up 11%), while Kenya posted the steepest decline among the big four markets, down 34%. Equity founders and equity investors did not leave Lagos for Nairobi in twelve months. Lenders did, and lenders were never in Lagos at scale to begin with.

An equity ranking measures what investors believe. A debt ranking measures what lenders can hedge. Kenya won on the second.

Why Debt Picks Currencies, Not Countries

A lender to an African startup has two ways to lend, and each one fails differently.

Hard-currency debt fails when the local currency moves. Nigeria's official rate was about N463 to N471 per dollar before the June 2023 float. By February 23, 2024 it was N1,665.50. A dollar loan serviced from naira receivables lost about 72% of its coverage in eight months. No covenant package survives that, and no credit committee approves the next one while the previous one is still being restructured.

Local-currency debt fails when the base rate is too high to leave a margin. The CBN's Monetary Policy Rate was 27% as recently as September 2025. A naira facility priced off that rate needs the borrower's unit economics to clear a hurdle that very few consumer or SME lending books in any market clear.

Kenya avoided both failures. The shilling hit a record low of about 162 per dollar in January 2024, then recovered after the February 2024 Eurobond buyback and has traded in a narrow band around 129 for almost two years, according to the Central Bank of Kenya's weekly bulletins. The CBK's Central Bank Rate stood at 8.75% in April 2026.

The clearest example is Sun King. In July 2025 it closed a KES 20.1 billion (about US$156 million) securitisation, arranged by Citi with Stanbic Bank Kenya as placement agent, which turns future customer repayments into investable shilling debt. That deal works because shilling repayments are predictable in shilling terms and shilling funding costs leave a margin. Run the same structure in naira at a 27% base rate and the numbers stop working before you even get to the credit risk.

It is the same currency-mismatch cost I called a liquidity tax in the stablecoin infrastructure piece, showing up on the liability side this time. Credit flows to the places where the currency a company collects in matches the currency it owes in. Kenya's PAYGo and asset-finance books happen to be the continent's largest pools of currency-matched collections.

The Variable That Just Changed

If the gap is about currency, then it should close when the currency stops moving. The currency has stopped moving.

According to CBN figures reported by the Nigerian financial press, the naira traded around N1,331 per dollar in mid-October 2026, stronger than its 2024 trough. Governor Cardoso said in December 2025 that the gap between the official and parallel rates had narrowed to under 2%, from more than 60%. Gross external reserves were US$55.25 billion on September 18, 2026.

On September 22, 2026, the MPC cut the Monetary Policy Rate by 350 basis points to 23%, the biggest cut since December 2006, saying the policy rate had drifted away from market rates. The next MPC meeting is today and tomorrow, November 23 and 24.

Equity investors are still pricing Nigeria off 2024's currency. Lenders will price it off 2026's currency first, because collateral and coverage ratios force them to.

This does not make Nigeria cheap, and it does not make Kenya fragile. A four-deal concentration is still a four-deal concentration, and a market whose lead depends on four structured facilities can lose it in one refinancing cycle. The point is narrower. The 2025 ranking is a reading of FX conditions over 2023 and 2024, published in 2026, and people are using it to make decisions about 2027.

The Copia Global retrospective made a related point about Kenya: a headline funding number tells you what was raised, not what the structure underneath can carry. The same reading works in reverse. A weak line on a funding table can show up after the risk behind it has already been repriced.

The IC Question

For any Nigerian portfolio company with a receivables book, the question for the next IC is not "why is Nigeria losing to Kenya?" It is this: at a 23% MPR and a naira that has held inside a narrow band for a year, could this company's collections support a local-currency facility today? If so, why is the plan to raise more dollar equity to fund working capital?

If the answer is yes and nobody has priced it, the company is overpaying for growth capital with the most expensive instrument it has. Run it through the Africa IC Checklist's currency dimension before the next round, not after.
