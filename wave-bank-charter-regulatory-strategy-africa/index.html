Wave Didn't Miss the PI-SPI Deadline. It Built a Way Around It.

The market is watching whether Wave connects to BCEAO's instant payment platform by September 30. That question was answered a year ago - just not on the roster anyone is checking.

On September 30, 2026, every WAEMU bank, e-money issuer, and payment institution must be connected to PI-SPI, the BCEAO's instant payment platform - the second deadline in three months after the central bank pushed the original June 30 date. Wave, the region's dominant mobile money operator, is still not on the published roster. Most of the coverage watching that date is asking the wrong question.

The Deadline Story, Again

I wrote in July about the first deadline extension and what it revealed about negotiating leverage in WAEMU fintech: Orange complied, Wave didn't, and the central bank blinked rather than force the issue. That read holds. September 30 is the next test of the same dynamic - extend again, sanction, or hold the line. But treating September 30 as the whole story misses something that happened thirteen months before this particular deadline existed.

The Bank Nobody Priced In

On August 4, 2025, Wave constituted Wave Bank Africa S.A. as a public limited company in Abidjan. It registered the entity on August 22, 2025, with starting capital of 20 billion CFA francs - roughly $35 million. Launch Base Africa's reporting at the time put that registration nine days ahead of the BCEAO's own September 1, 2025 deadline for payment institution licenses - one month before PI-SPI itself launched on September 30, 2025 with an initial roster of 31 institutions that excluded both Wave and MTN.

A mobile money operator about to be locked out of the region's new interoperability platform used that same month to file paperwork for a full banking license. That is not a coincidence of timing. It is a second regulatory track, opened before the first one closed.

Wave's response to exclusion from PI-SPI was not to wait for the next deadline. It was to build an institution the deadline doesn't govern.

Why a Charter Changes What the Deadline Means

A payment institution license and a banking license are not the same instrument with a different name. A bank charter authorizes deposit-taking and credit issuance - revenue that does not depend on transaction volume the way Wave's fee-based mobile money business does. It also carries a more direct relationship to central bank infrastructure than an e-money license does.

If the BCEAO grants Wave Bank Africa its agrement at or before September 30, the PI-SPI compliance question most coverage is tracking becomes close to irrelevant for the one operator everyone has been watching it for. Wave would be negotiating interoperability as a licensed bank with its own regulatory relationship to the central bank, not as a mobile money holdout waiting to be forced onto someone else's rails.

The Governance Signal, Not the Compliance One

The regulatory risk dimension in the Africa IC Checklist asks what happens when a deadline actually lands - not whether the current draft of a regulation applies to a portfolio company today. Most models of Wave's PI-SPI position treat it as a single-track bet: comply or don't, and the downside is a sanction or a forced connection. That model is now incomplete. Running a payment-institution compliance posture and a full banking license application in parallel, on a $35 million capital base, is not something most fintech challengers in the region have the balance sheet or the governance maturity to do at the same time - a pattern I traced through a different lens in the Wave war games retrospective, where the checklist would have flagged the regulatory bet as a strength, not a risk.

For anyone underwriting a WAEMU fintech deal against Wave as a competitive reference point, the operator edge here is not "will Wave comply with PI-SPI." It is whether the target company has, or could raise, the balance sheet to open a second regulatory track the way Wave just did - or whether its entire competitive position depends on staying inside a single license category that a larger regulatory shift could obsolete. The same exclusivity-versus-leverage pattern shows up in BCEAO Instruction 001-01-2024's telco-fintech provisions: the operators who move first on a parallel track are the ones who stop needing the regulator's permission on the original one.

What I Am Actually Watching

Whether the Commission Bancaire de l'UMOA grants Wave Bank Africa its agrement before, at, or after September 30, 2026. As of the most recent public reporting I could locate, that license had not yet been confirmed granted. If it lands before the deadline, the "will Wave comply" framing that has carried this story since last September stops being the interesting question. If it doesn't, Wave heads into a second missed deadline holding a bank charter application as a hedge nobody else on the roster has.
