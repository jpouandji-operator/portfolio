BCEAO's Deadline Ends Wave's Moat in Nine Days. Nobody Has Priced It.

The central bank's third PI-SPI connection deadline lands September 30. Wave still is not on the certified list. What an interoperability mandate does to a distribution moat built on closed rails — and why the adoption numbers complicate the obvious thesis.

On September 30, 2026, the BCEAO's connection deadline for PI-SPI — the WAEMU region's interoperable instant-payment platform — comes due for the second time this year. The first deadline, June 30, got pushed. This is the extension. As of the central bank's most recent published certification list, dated September 17, Wave is still not on it.

A Deadline Built to Not Be the Last One, Except This Time

The June 30 to September 30 push was not the first sign the BCEAO negotiates its own timelines. It is the second connection deadline since PI-SPI launched a year ago, on September 30, 2025. What is different this time is who got carved out and who did not. Banks, electronic money institutions, and payment institutions have until September 30. Microfinance institutions were given a separate deadline entirely — June 30, 2027. That is not the language of a regulator planning a blanket fourth extension. It is the language of a regulator closing one door while deliberately leaving another open for a category it has already decided needs more runway. Wave is not in the category that got more runway.

I am not treating "no more extensions" as confirmed. Nobody at the BCEAO has said that on the record, and I have flagged it below as something to verify directly against the central bank's own communique before this goes out. But the structure of the current deadline — a hard date for one category, a full year of additional runway for another — reads differently than a simple rollover.

What the September 17 List Actually Shows

The central bank's certified Business API list, updated September 17, carries 24 entries across the eight WAEMU member states. Senegal accounts for nine of them, Côte d'Ivoire eight. In five of the eight countries — Benin, Burkina Faso, Guinea-Bissau, Mali, and Togo — exactly one institution is certified: Ecobank. A separate, earlier roster of 104 institutions authorized to open PI-SPI services to the public, published July 31, is led by the same two countries and is composed overwhelmingly of banks, with telecom-owned mobile money subsidiaries and microfinance institutions filling the rest.

An "open, interoperable" platform where five of eight member countries have exactly one certified institution is not yet an open platform. It is a bank-led rail with an interoperability mandate attached, still waiting for the rest of the market to show up.

Wave does not appear on either list — not the July 31 authorization roster, not the September 17 certification list. That absence is not new. Wave was missing from the original validated participant list when PI-SPI launched a year ago, too. What has changed is the runway. A year of absence at launch was a company taking its time. Nine days of absence before a deadline the central bank appears to be treating as final is a company that has to decide, in public, whether closed distribution is still worth defending.

Why This Is a Moat Question, Not a Compliance Question

Wave built its Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire position on a closed network: your Wave balance moves to another Wave account, full stop. That closure is not an accident of infrastructure — it is the switching-cost mechanism the entire distribution thesis rests on. PI-SPI's whole design intent is to dissolve that mechanism by letting a Wave balance move to a bank account, an Orange Money wallet, or a Coris Bank account as easily as it moves within Wave today. Connect, and the walls that keep a Wave user inside Wave's ecosystem stop being walls.

That is the read behind treating September 30 as the date Wave's moat gets priced, not just a compliance date. Any fund holding a WAEMU mobile money position, or evaluating one, has been implicitly underwriting a closed-network assumption. If that assumption breaks on a specific, known date, the interesting question is not whether Wave connects. It is what the operator's own CAC and retention model does to the valuation the moment the rails open, whether or not Wave is the one that opens them.

The Adoption Numbers Complicate the Obvious Thesis

The easy version of this story is: interoperability mandate arrives, moat dissolves, distribution advantage evaporates overnight. The adoption data argues against the "overnight" part. By mid-July 2026, PI-SPI had processed roughly 1 million transactions worth about 110 billion CFA francs since launch — real, but a rounding error against tens of millions of active mobile money wallets already circulating in the zone. And as of 2025, cheques still accounted for 71.7% of clearing-system transaction value in WAEMU, even as their share of transaction volume had slipped to 24.6%. A platform that has moved a fraction of a percent of the region's payment value in its first year is not going to dissolve a distribution moat the week a deadline passes. It is going to erode it, unevenly, over a much longer runway than the deadline date implies.

The deadline is a regulatory event. The moat erosion is an adoption curve. Those two timelines are not the same, and pricing them as if they were is the mistake to watch for in the next round of WAEMU fintech diligence.

What I Am Actually Watching

September 30 is nine days out. I am watching one thing: whether Wave's name appears on the BCEAO's next published certification update, and if it does not, whether the central bank treats that absence differently than it treated the last one. A fourth extension would confirm Wave's leverage is still intact. A public enforcement action, even a symbolic one, would confirm the opposite. Either answer is a data point actual portfolios can use. The deadline date by itself is not.

If you are underwriting a WAEMU fintech's distribution position right now: ask the operator what happens to their CAC and 90-day retention model the day cross-rail transfers work, not the day PI-SPI launched or the day the deadline passed. If they have not run that model yet, that is the finding — not the deadline.
